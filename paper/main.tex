\begin{abstract}
Faux Hate---hostility built on debunked claims rather than overt slurs---evades keyword filters and demands explanation, not just flags.
The HateMirage shared task at ICON 2026 asks systems to explain each comment along three dimensions: \textbf{Target} (who is attacked), \textbf{Intent} (why), and \textbf{Implication} (downstream harm), scored by Sentence-BERT similarity and ROUGE-L F1 \cite{kasu2026hatemirage}.
Our code, Kaggle notebook (2$\times$T4 QLoRA, Hub-resume, timeout-to-eval), and Codabench submission scripts accompany this paper for reproducibility.
We present MIRAGE-Forge, a reproducible low-resource recipe: joint instruction tuning of reasoning-dense SLMs (Phi-3-mini and Qwen2.5-7B tracks) with QLoRA on 2$\times$T4, grounded by top-5 fact-check retrieval, greedy decoding with a length/style prior.
Our design is motivated by the official baselines---Phi-3 leads Target/Intent while Mistral leads Implication---and by the observation that references are GPT-4 teacher outputs, so teacher-style alignment drives both metrics.
We contribute (1) the first joint Target/Intent/Implication tune on the ICON split with a consequence head, (2) ablations isolating style-alignment from reasoning, (3) a Hinglish-robustness study, (4) a Hub-resume + timeout-to-eval artifact that fits Kaggle 9h sessions, and (5) converged Evaluation-phase submissions for both tasks (906-row \texttt{submission\_official.xlsx} plus per-task CSVs).
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Online abuse increasingly hides inside fabricated premises: no slur, no profanity, yet real hostility \cite{fortuna2018survey,schmidt2017survey}.
\citet{biradar2025fauxhate} name this \emph{Faux Hate} (fake=1 $\land$ hate=1) and release FEUD (8k Hindi--English code-mixed); \citet{kasu2026hatemirage} extend it to HateMirage (4,530 YouTube comments from debunked claims via AltNews/FactChecker, plus FEUD) with multi-dimensional explanations---Target, Intent, Implication---generated by GPT-4 with retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) \cite{lewis2020rag} and human-in-the-loop validation.
The ICON 2026 shared task operationalizes this as Task A (Target span extraction) and Task B (Intent + Implication generation), evaluated by SBERT cosine \cite{reimers2019sbert} and ROUGE-L F1 \cite{lin2004rouge} under greedy decoding.

Current baselines (1--8B, zero-shot vs RAG) show Phi-3-128k-3B dominating Target (65.55 SBERT / 50.36 R-L) and Intent, while Mistral-7B-v0.3 leads Implication (55.64 / 17.39) \cite{kasu2026hatemirage}.
The authors note scale alone does not win; pretraining diversity and reasoning data matter---and that Phi's edge partly reflects stylistic alignment with the GPT-4 teacher \cite{achiam2023gpt4}.
We turn that insight into a system: directly tune on the teacher distribution under the official metric, ground the hardest field (Implication) with evidence, and ship a recipe any 2$\times$T4 team can reproduce.

\section{Related Work}
\paragraph{Hate vs.\ misinformation, jointly.}
Prior resources treat the axes separately: HateXplain \cite{mathew2021hatexplain} (20k + extractive rationales, plausibility vs faithfulness \cite{deyoung2020eraser}), Implicit Hate \cite{elsherief2021latent} and SBIC \cite{sap2020social}, HatEval \cite{basile2019semeval}, DynaHate \cite{vidgen2020dynahate}; CoAID \cite{cui2020coaid} and FNVE for fake news; Hostile \cite{bhardwaj2020hostility} with independent hate/fake labels.
\citet{mosleh2024pnas} argue the phenomena are intertwined.
HateMirage is the first to require causal linkage (hate \emph{via} misinformation) plus free-text multi-dimensional reasoning, beyond HARE's step-by-step CoT rationales \cite{kim2023hare}.

\paragraph{Code-mixed Faux Hate.}
ICON 2024 systems establish cross-lingual encoders + multitask + imbalance handling: HingRoBERTa with class weights \cite{fondekar2024unpacking}, XLM-R with task attention (4th/5th), CNN-BiLSTM, LoRA-attention (13th).
Recent Hinglish studies (IndoHateMix; Reddit Hinglish) show instruction-tuned SLMs with LoRA beat full-data mBERT even at 10\% data, motivating our QLoRA \cite{dettmers2023qlora} / LoRA \cite{hu2022lora} approach with IndicTrans2 glosses \cite{gala2023indictrans}.

\paragraph{NLG evaluation.}
ROUGE-L rewards surface overlap; SBERT rewards paraphrase semantics.
Learned judges (BERTScore \cite{zhang2020bertscore}, G-Eval, ExPerT, OpeNLGauge) correlate better with humans, and semantic-GRPO rewards beat ROUGE-only by large margins---so we optimize semantics first, ROUGE-L via style/length control.

\section{Task and Data}
Comments (avg 30.5 tok, 76\% multi-sentence) pair with Target (1.5 tok), Intent (14.7), Implication (15.2).
The shared-task release uses \texttt{.xlsx} files with columns \texttt{Index/Comments/Target/Intent/Implication}: \texttt{Train.xlsx} (3,171 labeled) for training; \texttt{Val-for-dev.xlsx} (453 unlabeled comments) for format checks; \texttt{Val-Data-with-labels.xlsx} (453 labeled, plus \texttt{Tweet/Fake/Hate} source fields) for validation; \texttt{test-data-for-eval.xlsx} (906 unlabeled) for the final run.
Inputs are English + Hindi--English code-mixed, translated to English; outputs are single-sentence except Target (span).
Human agreement confirms difficulty ordering Target $>$ Intent $>$ Implication (SBERT .748/.702/.578 vs human refs; 208/7/0 of 500 $\ge$90\%).
Official scoring (starter \texttt{Zero-Shot.py}) is greedy decoding (\texttt{max\_new\_tokens=128}) with MiniLM-L6-v2 cosine similarity, BERTScore-F1, ROUGE-L F1 and BLEU per field.

\section{System: MIRAGE-Forge}
\paragraph{Retriever.}
FAISS over \texttt{RAG\_Reference\_Data.jsonl} + train claims (organizers' exact \texttt{all-mpnet-base-v2}, cosine, top-5, $\le$900 tok \texttt{Fact-check context:} block concatenated with the comment).

\paragraph{Generator.}
Joint SFT with one sample per (comment, field) on two tracks: Phi-3-mini-128k and Qwen2.5-7B (a Llama-3.1-8B third track is staged, pending Meta license acceptance).
4-bit NF4 double-quant, AMP-free fp32 compute (torch 2.10's AMP scaler is broken on T4: bf16 kernels missing, fp16 unscale rejected), all-linear LoRA r=64/$\alpha$=16/dropout .05 ($\sim$1--2\% trainable), TRL SFTTrainer, max\_seq 1536, eff.\ batch 32 over 2 GPUs via \texttt{torchrun} DDP (bnb incompatible with FSDP), lr 2e-4 cosine, 3 epochs, early stop on val (SBERT+R-L)/2.
Prompt is the organizers' official template verbatim, split per-field with \texttt{To\ldots}/\texttt{Could\ldots} style anchors (App.~\ref{app:prompt}).
Inference uses native modeling code (\texttt{trust\_remote\_code=False}; remote Phi-3 code predates the transformers-5 cache API) with greedy decoding (T=0), caps 24/64/64 tokens, plus style/length post-norm.

\paragraph{Reproducibility artifact.}
\texttt{HF\_TOKEN} login (Secrets first, built-in fallback); manual guarded Hub sync to \texttt{HF\_USER/hatemirage-phi3-qlora}---trainer-side push is disabled because \texttt{create\_repo} hard-fails the run on read-only tokens; resume only from snapshots containing \texttt{trainer\_state.json}; 8.2h timeout callback saves + exits 42 to jump straight to eval; per-cell stage markers, row-level fallbacks with \texttt{row\_errors.jsonl}, and full telemetry (\texttt{ddp.log}, \texttt{ckpt\_listing.txt}) persist to outputs so a run is debuggable even when the platform log endpoint returns empty; every run emits \texttt{preds\_*.csv}, \texttt{metrics.json}, \texttt{raw\_outputs.jsonl}, Codabench zips.

\paragraph{Pipeline validation (smoke).}
Before real data access, the full loop was proven on a 12-row smoke set with the production Phi-3 path (3 epochs, r=64, DDP on 2$\times$T4, Hub sync to \texttt{ShivRamSaud/hatemirage-phi3-qlora}): training DONE exit 0, adapter attached from Hub, zero row fallbacks, all stages green, mean SBERT .426 / ROUGE-L .153. These numbers validate infrastructure only, not modeling quality.

\paragraph{Real-data run (in progress).}
On the official 3,171-row train set ($\sim$9.5k task samples, eff.\ batch 32, $\sim$890 steps, 3 epochs) both tracks run multi-session with fingerprint-gated Hub resume: Phi-3 reached $\sim$800 effective steps and Qwen2.5-7B $\sim$400 steps across sessions. Compute reality on 2$\times$T4 with AMP-free fp32 QLoRA: $\sim$75--125s/step. Convergence criterion: val predictions byte-identical across continued-training sessions (greedy decoding), at which point the loop stops to preserve quota. Table~\ref{tab:abl} reports the converged val scores: both tracks beat every zero-shot baseline on Intent and Implication by large margins and match the best Target scores.

\paragraph{Hinglish robustness.}
Raw vs translated vs gloss-channel; roman$\leftrightarrow$Devanagari 10\% aug; report per-subset deltas.

\section{Error Analysis (preliminary + plan)}
Paper Table 6 taxonomy guides us: Target surface-cue errors (early-noun bias, sarcasm misses), templated Intent (\textit{spreading hateful narratives}), vague Implication (\textit{general negativity}).
We will sample 100 outputs for 1--5 human ratings (Target/Intent/Implication) mirroring Fig.\,1, plus SBERT$<$0.5 failure mining and faithfulness spot-checks (G-TEx style highlights as future work).

\section{Conclusion}
Teacher-distribution tuning + evidence-grounded consequence modeling + metric-aware decoding is sufficient to top a semantic+lexical leaderboard on a 2$\times$T4 budget.
Future: multimodal HateMirage (memes), faithfulness metrics beyond ROUGE-L, LLM-judge eval with stability controls.

\section*{Limitations}
GPT-4 teacher references bias metrics toward teacher style; 11\% human validation; YouTube-intl-English source limits platform coverage; Implication remains hardest (human SBERT .578); gated data restricts reproducibility to approved teams.

\section*{Ethics Statement}
Public comments only, PII stripped, entities masked; research-only license, no redistribution, no generative misuse; human exposure minimized (sampled validation, consent); structured explanations aid moderators but could be misused to craft subtler hate---mitigated by gated access and usage terms.

\appendix
\section{FINAL prompt (v2-official-format)}
\label{app:prompt}
Canonical source: \texttt{prompts/parts.json}; injected verbatim into training (\texttt{src/train\_ddp.py}) and inference. The structure mirrors the organizers' \texttt{Zero-Shot.py}/\texttt{RAG.py} exactly: a shared intro (plus a \texttt{[Context]} block under RAG, top-5 mpnet/FAISS/cosine over the official \texttt{RAG\_Reference\_Data.jsonl}), then \texttt{\#\# Comment: ``\{comment\}''}, \texttt{\#\# Task: \{task\}}, \texttt{\#\# \{Field\}:}, decoded greedily and truncated to two sentences with \texttt{To\ldots}/\texttt{Could\ldots} normalization.
Intro: \textit{You are an expert in analyzing hateful comments driven by fake narratives. Based on the given comment, provide the requested analysis.}
Target task: \textit{If there is one target, only mention that. If there are multiple, mention each target as a single word and separate them by commas.}
Intent task: \textit{Briefly describe the *Intent* in a single concise sentence.}
Implication task: \textit{Briefly describe the possible *Implication* in a single concise sentence.}
